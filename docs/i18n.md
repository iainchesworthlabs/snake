# Translations

The source language is English. The user interface also ships in Simplified Chinese (`zh_CN`), French, Spanish and
German, and Japanese; Hebrew and Yiddish follow. Strings are
written with `qsTr` in QML and `tr` in C++, and the catalogues are Qt Linguist files in
`apps/snake/translations/snake_<code>.ts`. They are compiled into the executable, and
`main.cpp` loads the one that matches the system language, or the one named by `--language <code>` (`snake --language
de`), which also sets how numbers are written. A language without a catalogue shows the English text.

| Language | File | Origin | Review |
|---|---|---|---|
| English (`en`) | `snake_en.ts` | Source text copied | Not needed |
| Simplified Chinese (`zh_CN`) | `snake_zh_CN.ts` | Machine translation | Not yet read by a speaker |
| French (`fr`) | `snake_fr.ts` | Machine translation | Not yet read by a speaker |
| Spanish (`es`) | `snake_es.ts` | Machine translation | Not yet read by a speaker |
| German (`de`) | `snake_de.ts` | Machine translation | Not yet read by a speaker |
| Japanese (`ja`) | `snake_ja.ts` | Machine translation | Not yet read by a speaker |

## Changing a string

1. Edit the `qsTr` or `tr` text.
2. Run `cmake --build --preset <preset> --target update_translations`. It rewrites the `.ts` files and marks
   new or changed strings as unfinished.
3. Fill in each unfinished translation and remove the `type="unfinished"` attribute.

The unit test `translations` fails when a language lacks a source string, when an entry is unfinished,
obsolete or empty, and when a translation does not keep the placeholders (`%1`, `%n`) of its source.

## Adding a language

Add `translations/snake_<code>.ts` to `qt_add_translations` in `apps/snake/CMakeLists.txt` and to `LANGUAGES` in
`apps/snake/tests/test_translations.cpp`, then follow the steps above. The standard also lists French,
Spanish, German, Hebrew, Yiddish and Japanese. Hebrew and Yiddish need right-to-left layout and a font with
Hebrew coverage; that work starts when the window has text beyond a title.

## Words that are not translated

The name of the game, Snake, is the same in every language but Chinese. Names typed on the leaderboard are the letters A to Z
and a space in every language, as the requirements say.

## Making a screen fit

German and French words are longer than English ones, and the tests check every screen in every language for text that
runs out of its button, card or panel, or off the page (`The title, the settings and the leaderboard fit in every
language` and its partner in `test_screens.cpp`). A label that is too wide for its button is set smaller, down to 9
pixels (`ToyButton`); a heading is set smaller, down to a third (`ShadowText`); the detail under a mode wraps to two
lines (`ModeCard`). If a translation still does not fit, shorten the translation before adding more code. The test adds a
language through its `TESTED_LANGUAGES` list.

## Fonts

Pixelify Sans, the game's font, has Latin letters only (with the accents of French, Spanish and German). The other
scripts use Noto Sans, under the SIL Open Font License: Noto Sans JP for Japanese, Noto Sans SC for Simplified Chinese,
and Noto Sans Hebrew for Hebrew and Yiddish. Each is cut down to the characters that the translations use, Regular and
Bold, by `tools/fonts/subset_fonts.py` (it needs `pip install fonttools brotli`, downloads the source fonts and checks
their SHA-256), and the results are in `apps/snake/fonts/` as WOFF2 files of 25 to 40 KB. The licence text is in
`LICENSE-noto-sans.txt` and in the About screen. The font of the language comes first for the Han characters that
Japanese and Chinese share, because the two draw them differently (`application_font()`).

When a translation gains a character that no bundled font has, the test `the bundled fonts cover every character of every
translation` fails and names the language and the character. Run the script again and commit the fonts.
